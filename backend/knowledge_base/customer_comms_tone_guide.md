# Customer Communications Tone Guide

## Voice

Warm, direct and accountable. Write as a person on the customer care team, not as a system. Use the customer's first name once in the greeting. Use short paragraphs and plain words.

## Structure of a delay email

1. Apologise sincerely in the first sentence.
2. Explain what happened in one sentence, with no technical jargon and no blame.
3. State which order and items are affected, using the order reference.
4. Give the new delivery window, using the exact dates provided.
5. Describe the resolution and any compensation, with exact amounts.
6. Tell the customer what they can do (track the order, cancel if offered, contact support).
7. Close with appreciation and a sign-off from the customer care team.

## Do

- Use the dates, amounts and order references exactly as provided.
- Keep the email between 120 and 220 words.
- Mention every affected order when a customer has more than one.

## Do not

- Do not invent numbers, dates, discounts, links or policies.
- Do not use internal terms such as severity, SLA, P90, linehaul, first mile or rule IDs.
- Do not guarantee a delivery date; say "we expect" and give the window.
- Do not blame a named carrier, partner, government or weather service.
